Five rules before you buy

  1. Measure before you look at chairs. Height, weight and hours per day. Without that, any ranking is worth the same to you — that is, nothing. ErgoCheck calculates it for you in one minute.
  2. The seat range rules. If your popliteal height falls outside the range, no other spec makes up for it: your feet dangle or your knees ride up.
  3. At 6+ h/day, adjustable or active lumbar. It's the criterion that most separates an entry-level chair from a mid-range one.
  4. The warranty is information. 12 years (Steelcase, Herman Miller, Hinomi) versus 2 years says a lot about what the manufacturer expects of its product.
  5. Distrust uniform 5-star ratings. We don't display ratings we can't verify; we score specs with a published method.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know what seat height I need?

Your popliteal height (floor to the back of the knee) is about 25% of your height. The chair must cover that value within its seat range. At 1.75 m you need ~44 cm: check that the manufacturer's range includes it. The chair filter does it automatically.

Is adjustable lumbar support worth it?

If you sit 6 or more hours a day, yes: a fixed lumbar rarely lands exactly on your curve. Adjustable lets you position it; active (self-adapting) follows you as you move.

What is EN 1335 certification?

The European office chair standard: minimum dimensions, stability and durability verified in a lab. It doesn't guarantee the chair will suit you, but it does guarantee it isn't a toy.

Gaming chair or ergonomic chair?

Most gaming chairs prioritize looks over adjustment (bucket-style backrest, cushion lumbar). The serious exceptions, like Secretlab, are in our catalog, scored with the same method as the rest.

They are indicative, with a capture date. The current price is always the store's: check it before deciding.
