How to choose

01Separating your hands to shoulder width

That is the point of every ergonomic keyboard. A fixed split gives you most of the benefit with no learning curve; a two-piece split lets you put each hand exactly where your shoulder wants it.

02Dropping the number pad

Without a number pad the mouse comes closer to the centre of your body and the right shoulder rotates less. If you enter data daily, a separate pad solves both.

03Tenting and negative tilt

Raising the inner edge turns the palms slightly towards each other and reduces forearm rotation. Negative tilt, with the keyboard sloping away from you, lowers wrist extension.

04Mechanism and noise

Scissor keys are the quietest. Among mechanical switches, soft linears tire the fingers less and light tactiles signal the actuation point without forcing you to bottom out.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to adapt to a split keyboard?

With a curved split, a few days. With a full split in two halves, one to two weeks until you get your usual typing speed back.

Full split or curved to start with?

If you're coming from a flat keyboard, the curved one delivers most of the postural benefit with barely any learning curve. The full split is the next step if you need to separate your hands to the exact width of your shoulders.

Does the keyboard matter more than the mouse?

If you type for many hours, yes: the keyboard determines your shoulder and wrist posture most of the time. Ideally you fix both, starting with whichever you use most.
