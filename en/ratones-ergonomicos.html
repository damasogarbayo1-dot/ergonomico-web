Choosing an ergonomic mouse by hand size

The most expensive mistake when buying an ergonomic mouse is not choosing the wrong brand: it is choosing the wrong size. A premium vertical mouse that does not fit your hand works worse than a simple one that does.

How to measure your hand in 20 seconds

  1. Rest your open, relaxed hand on the desk, palm down.
  2. Measure with a ruler from the wrist crease to the tip of the middle finger.
  3. Compare the result with the table: that is your size.
Hand size and what to look for in each case
Wrist→middle fingerSizeWhat to look for
Under 17 cmSCompact vertical mice (length ≈ 105-115 mm). Large vertical mice force you to stretch your fingers and lose the postural advantage.
Between 17 and 19 cmMThe most widely covered size on the market: almost every vertical mouse and trackball is designed for this hand.
Over 19 cmLLong bodies (≈ 120-135 mm) so the whole palm rests. With small mice you will end up gripping with your fingers only.

What is checked

  • Size compatibility with the dimensions declared by the manufacturer.
  • Dominant hand: a real left-handed version or an ambidextrous design, not "suitable for both".
  • Vertical angle: 57-60° is the range that really neutralises forearm pronation. Below that, the correction is marginal.

Frequently asked questions

How do you measure hand size to choose a mouse?

Rest your open hand on the desk and measure with a ruler from the wrist crease to the tip of the middle finger. Under 17 cm is size S, between 17 and 19 cm is M and over 19 cm is L. Twenty seconds that avoid the most common wrong purchase.

Vertical, ergonomic or trackball mouse?

The vertical mouse (57-60° tilt) is the most neutral posture for the forearm and the best choice if you have wrist discomfort. The trackball avoids moving the arm, useful with little space or if the pain comes from the shoulder. The classic ergonomic mouse gives comfort without changing the gesture you are used to.

How long does it take to get used to a vertical mouse?

Between two days and two weeks of normal use. For office work adaptation is quick; for precision work (design, editing) it usually takes a bit longer.

I'm left-handed — what are my options?

Few but good: there are vertical mice with a specific left-hand version and ambidextrous trackballs. The dominant-hand filter on this page shows you only the compatible ones.

Does an ergonomic mouse cure wrist pain?

It reduces postural load, but it does not replace breaks or a correct desk and chair height. If the pain persists, see a healthcare professional.
