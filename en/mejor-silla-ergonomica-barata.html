How to choose

01The seat height range rules

Your popliteal height is around 25% of your total height and has to fall inside the range the manufacturer states. At 1.75 m that is about 44 cm: a chair with a 45 to 55 cm range is already too high for you, and no other spec fixes that.

02Lumbar support: fixed, adjustable or active

A fixed lumbar rarely lands where your curve wants it. Adjustable lets you set it at your height and forget it; active or self-adjusting follows you as you move and suits you better if you shift position a lot.

03Seat depth and armrests

Two or three fingers should fit between the edge of the seat and the back of your knee. And the armrests must drop far enough to leave your elbows at 90° over the desk: if they don't, the weight of your forearm lands on your trapezius.

04Maximum load and warranty

The load is a test limit, not a recommendation: leave 20% of margin. The warranty is the best clue to real durability, because it is what the manufacturer is willing to pay if it fails.

Frequently asked questions

What do you give up in an entry-level chair?

Usually adjustable seat depth, 4D armrests and long warranties. Adjustable lumbar support is within reach in this bracket, and it's the first thing you should insist on.

Is a second-hand high-end chair worth it?

It's usually the best buy per euro spent if the model has spare parts available and you check the condition of the gas lift and the mechanism. Models like the Aeron or the Leap V2 are designed to last more than a decade.

A cheap chair now or wait for a better one?

If you sit for many hours, a cheap chair with adjustable lumbar today is better than a bad chair for a year. The chair filter tells you which ones cover your measurements within your budget.
